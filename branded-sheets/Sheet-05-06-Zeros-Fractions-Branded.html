Term 1

Factorization Rules — Quick Reference (Term 1)

First Term — New Curriculum
RULES — PURPLE

All polynomials in standard descending form over $\mathbb{R}$. Factor completely:

1. GCF: Look first for a common factor in all terms. Take it out.
Formal: $ax+ay=a(x+y)$

2. Difference of squares: Two squares with minus between. Two brackets, one plus one minus.
Formal: $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$

3. Perfect square: First and last are squares and middle is double product. One bracket squared.
Formal: $a^2\pm 2ab+b^2=(a\pm b)^2$

4. Trinomial $x^2+bx+c$: Find two numbers that multiply to last and add to middle.
Formal: find $m\cdot n=c$ and $m+n=b$, then $x^2+bx+c=(x+m)(x+n)$

5. Trinomial $ax^2+bx+c$ by grouping / ac: Multiply a*c, split middle, group.
Formal: find $p\cdot q=a\cdot c$ and $p+q=b$, split and group

6. Sum / difference of cubes: Cube minus/plus cube. Small bracket times big bracket.
Formal: $a^3\pm b^3=(a\pm b)(a^2\mp ab+b^2)$

7. Grouping: Group pairs, take common, then take common bracket.
Formal: group terms with a common factor, then factor again

EXAMPLES — GREEN
Q: 1. GCF — Factor $6x^2-2x^3-4x$ completely.
$$6x^2-2x^3-4x=-2x^3+6x^2-4x$$$$=-2x(x^2-3x+2)$$$$=\boxed{-2x(x-1)(x-2)}$$
Q: 2. Difference of squares — Factor $x^2-16$ and $-9x^2+25$.
$$x^2-16=x^2-4^2$$$$=\boxed{(x-4)(x+4)}$$$$-9x^2+25=25-9x^2=5^2-(3x)^2$$$$=\boxed{(5-3x)(5+3x)}$$
Q: 3. Perfect square — Factor $x^2-2ax+a^2$.
$$x^2-2ax+a^2$$$$=\boxed{(x-a)^2}$$
Q: 4. Trinomial — Factor $x^2-3x-4$.
$$x^2-3x-4$$$$m \cdot n=-4 ,\; m+n=-3 \Rightarrow m=-4 ,\; n=1$$$$=\boxed{(x-4)(x+1)}$$
Q: 5. Grouping / ac — Factor $2x^2-5x-12$.
$$2x^2-5x-12$$$$=2x^2+3x-8x-12$$$$=x(2x+3)-4(2x+3)$$$$=\boxed{(2x+3)(x-4)}$$
Q: 6. Cubes — Factor $x^3-125$ and $2x^4+54x$.
$$x^3-125=x^3-5^3$$$$=\boxed{(x-5)(x^2+5x+25)}$$$$2x^4+54x=2x(x^3+27)$$$$=2x(x^3+3^3)$$$$=\boxed{2x(x+3)(x^2-3x+9)}$$
Q: 7. Grouping — Factor $2x^4+x^3-6x^2$.
$$2x^4+x^3-6x^2=x^2(2x^2+x-6)$$$$=x^2(2x^2+4x-3x-6)$$$$=x^2[2x(x+2)-3(x+2)]$$$$=\boxed{x^2(2x-3)(x+2)}$$

Set of Zeros of a Polynomial Function — Key Theory

DEFINITION — BLUE

Generally: If $f$ is a polynomial function in $x$, then the set of values of $x$ for which $f(x)=0$ is called the set of zeros of the function $f$.

$$Z(f)=\{x \in \mathbb{R} : f(x)=0\}$$

It is also the solution set of the equation $f(x)=0$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

Notice: $f$ denotes the function, $f(x)$ denotes the rule or the image, and $Z(f)$ denotes the set of zeros.
RULE — HOW TO FIND ZEROS

To find $Z(f)$: set $f(x)=0$, factorize in standard descending order, then collect all real values that satisfy the equation. A constant non-zero function has $Z(f)=\varnothing$. The zero function has $Z(f)=\mathbb{R}$.

Algebraic Fractional Function — Key Theory

DEFINITION — BLUE

If $p$ and $k$ are two polynomial functions, and $Z(k)$ is the set of zeroes of $k$, then

$$n: \mathbb{R} - Z(k) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad n(x) = \dfrac{p(x)}{k(x)}$$

is called a real algebraic fractional function. Its domain is $\mathbb{R}$ minus the zeroes of the denominator.

RULE — ZEROES OF n

The set of zeroes of $n$ = values making the numerator zero and the denominator non-zero.

$$Z(n) = Z(\text{numerator}) - Z(\text{denominator})$$

EXAMPLE — GREEN
Q: (a) Find $Z(n)$ for $n(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3x}{x^2-9}$.
$$n(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3x}{x^2-9}$$$$=\dfrac{x(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}$$$$Z(\text{numerator})=\{0,-3\} ,\; Z(\text{denominator})=\{3,-3\}$$$$Z(n)=\{0,-3\}-\{3,-3\}$$Ans: $\boxed{\{0\}}$
Q: (b) Find $Z(n)$ for $n(x)=\dfrac{3x+6}{x^2+x-2}$.
$$n(x)=\dfrac{3x+6}{x^2+x-2}$$$$=\dfrac{3(x+2)}{(x-1)(x+2)}$$$$Z(\text{numerator})=\{-2\} ,\; Z(\text{denominator})=\{1,-2\}$$$$Z(n)=\{-2\}-\{1,-2\}$$Ans: $\boxed{\varnothing}$
RULE — COMMON DOMAIN

The common domain of two algebraic fractions or more = $\mathbb{R}$ minus the zeroes of all denominators.

Q: Find the common domain of $n_1(x)=\dfrac{3}{x-2}$ and $n_2(x)=\dfrac{5x}{x^2-1}$.
$$x-2=0 \Rightarrow x=2$$$$m_1=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}$$$$x^2-1=0 \Rightarrow x=1 ,\; x=-1$$$$m_2=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1,-1\}$$Ans: common domain $=\boxed{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2,1,-1\}}$
