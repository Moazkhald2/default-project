Algebraic Fractional Function — Key Theory (p. 23)

DEFINITION — BLUE

If $p$ and $k$ are two polynomial functions, and $Z(k)$ is the set of zeroes of $k$, then

$$n: \mathbb{R} - Z(k) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad n(x) = \frac{p(x)}{k(x)}$$

is called a real algebraic fractional function. Its domain is $\mathbb{R}$ minus the zeroes of the denominator.

RULE — ZEROES OF n

The set of zeroes of $n$ = values making the numerator zero and the denominator non-zero.

$$Z(n) = Z(\text{numerator}) - Z(\text{denominator})$$

EXAMPLE — GREEN

(a) $n(x) = \dfrac{x^2+3x}{x^2-9} = \dfrac{x(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}$

$Z(n) = \{0,-3\} - \{3,-3\} = \{0\}$

(b) $n(x) = \dfrac{3x+6}{x^2+x-2} = \dfrac{3(x+2)}{(x-1)(x+2)}$

$Z(n) = \{-2\} - \{1,-2\} = \varnothing$

RULE — COMMON DOMAIN

The common domain of two algebraic fractions or more = $\mathbb{R}$ minus the zeroes of all denominators.

Example: $n_1(x)=\dfrac{3}{x-2}$, $n_2(x)=\dfrac{5x}{x^2-1}$. Then $m_1 = \mathbb{R}-\{2\}$, $m_2 = \mathbb{R}-\{1,-1\}$, so common domain $= \mathbb{R}-\{2,1,-1\}$.

Equality of Two Algebraic Functions — Theory (p. 27)

DEFINITION — REDUCING

Reducing an algebraic fraction to its simplest form means dividing both numerator and denominator by their highest common factor (HCF).

RULE — 3 STEPS
  1. Factorize the numerator and the denominator.
  2. Identify the domain before cancelling.
  3. Remove the common factors.
DEFINITION — EQUALITY

Two algebraic functions $n_1, n_2$ are equal iff:

$$\text{domain}_1 = \text{domain}_2 \quad \text{AND} \quad n_1(x) = n_2(x)\ \ \forall\, x \in \text{common domain}$$
